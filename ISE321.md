# Methodology for P-Measure Forecasting of Autocallable Structured Products

---

## 1. Introduction

Structured Products are hybrid investments that link returns to the performance of an underlying asset. This project outlines a methodology for estimating the expected payoff of Autocallable Structured Notes using a "real-world" forecasting approach.

Unlike standard valuation methods that seek a fair market price based on risk-neutral assumptions, this engine forecasts a range of potential outcomes based on historical, real-world data patterns. The core of this engine utilizes a specialized Monte Carlo simulation technique designed to handle the distribution characteristics of barrier products.

---

## 2. Forecasting Approach: The P-Measure

A critical distinction defines the mechanics of this model: the difference between pricing an asset and forecasting its behavior.

* **Q-Measure (Risk-Neutral):** Typically used to determine fair pricing. It assumes the expected return of all assets equates to the risk-free rate, neutralizing individual asset risk preferences.
* **P-Measure (Real-World):** Used here to forecast actual expected behavior. It incorporates the asset's specific historical drift and volatility profiles to project future paths.

To achieve a consistent P-Measure forecast, the model adjusts two primary parameters relative to standard pricing engines.

### 2.1 Model Parameters

#### 2.1.1 Historical Drift
The simulation does not utilize the risk-free rate for its drift component. Instead, it calibrates the drift based on the asset's historical log returns. The model estimates the annualized mean of these returns and converts them into an arithmetic mean suitable for the simulation engine. This ensures the projected asset paths reflect the asset's actual historical growth trajectory rather than a theoretical risk-free baseline.

#### 2.1.2 Consistent Discounting
For the forecast to remain internally consistent, the rate used to discount future payoffs must align with the growth assumptions used in the simulation. If the model simulates paths using a specific historical drift, it must discount the resulting payoffs using a commensurate rate. This alignment prevents the artificial inflation of present value that would occur if high-growth paths were discounted at a lower, risk-free rate.

## 3. Validation Summary

To evaluate the methodology, the model was tested against historical market outcomes across a diverse set of underlyings, including equity indices, commodities, and industry-specific ETFs.

The validation process focused on two primary dimensions:
1.  **Standardized Structures:** Testing across a broad basket of assets using uniform contract terms to isolate asset-specific performance.
2.  **Real-World Structures:** Testing against specific issuer filings to verify the model's handling of complex, tailored contract terms (varying barriers, coupons, and observation dates).

**General Observations:**
The methodology demonstrates that "real-world" forecasting aligns closely with product design when the simulation parameters—specifically the volatility and drift assumptions—are tailored to the specific structural constraints of the note. The results highlight that accurate forecasting requires a strict alignment between the simulation's statistical inputs and the legal specifications of the product's barrier and coupon logic. The model achieved an absolute mean percentage error of 19% on standardized structures, showing sensitivity to homogenous contract architecture. On the other hand, the model achieved an absolute mean error of 8.32% on real structured note contracts. These results confirm the model consistently prices notes in a stable manner.
